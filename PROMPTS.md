## 1. LAND CLIENTS

### 1. Cold Outreach for [Your Service]

**Use when:** You need to reach out to a prospect cold and want copy that lands, not lands in spam.

```
I sell [YOUR SERVICE] to [TARGET AUDIENCE].
One specific problem I solve: [PROBLEM].
The result my clients see: [RESULT/OUTCOME].

Write me a 2-sentence cold email hook that:
- Opens with a specific observation about THEIR business (not me)
- Names the problem I solve
- Has a soft call-to-action (link to calendar, a reply, a resource)

Keep it under 50 words. Make it feel like it's from a human who did their homework.
```

### 2. LinkedIn Positioning Statement

**Use when:** Your profile needs one sentence that makes prospects stop scrolling and think "that's me."

```
I help [TARGET AUDIENCE] achieve [SPECIFIC OUTCOME] by [YOUR UNIQUE METHOD/ANGLE].

My ideal client is someone who [DESCRIBES THEIR PAIN/SITUATION].

Write me a 1-sentence positioning statement for my LinkedIn headline (max 60 chars) and a 3-sentence "About" section that:
- Leads with the outcome they care about, not my title
- Builds credibility (proof, not claims)
- Ends with a reason to reach out

Make it sound like a peer, not a pitch.
```

### 3. Case Study Teaser for Cold List

**Use when:** You want to spark curiosity in a batch of prospects who don't know you yet.

```
I just finished a project with [CLIENT TYPE/INDUSTRY]:
- Their problem was: [SPECIFIC CHALLENGE]
- What we did: [SOLUTION/APPROACH]
- The result: [METRIC/OUTCOME]

Write me a short email (100 words max) that tells this story and ends with: "Curious if this could work for your team?"

Make the teaser so specific they think "wait, are they talking about us?"
```

### 4. Automated Lead-Gen Question Sequence

**Use when:** You want to qualify leads fast without a 30-min call that goes nowhere.

```
I sell [SERVICE] and get a lot of inbound interest, but many aren't a fit.

Here are the key qualifiers I need to know:
1. [QUALIFIER 1 — e.g., budget, timeline, use case]
2. [QUALIFIER 2]
3. [QUALIFIER 3]

Write me a 3-question sequence I can send via email/Slack that feels conversational (not a survey), gets me the info I need to disqualify fast, and leaves a good impression even if they're not a fit.
```

### 5. Referral Bait: The "You Know Someone Who" Message

**Use when:** You want referral momentum without begging.

```
I just helped [CLIENT] with [SPECIFIC OUTCOME].

Before that, I worked with [SIMILAR CLIENT TYPE].

Write me a message I can send to past clients and warm contacts that:
- Describes the exact type of person/business I'm looking to meet (be very specific)
- Explains why they'd benefit from what I do (so they can spot it)
- Offers something small in return (intro on a call, free resource, etc.)
- Asks them to introduce me if they know someone (not pressure, just permission)

Keep it friendly. No desperation.
```

### 6. Niche Positioning: The "I Only Work With" Statement

**Use when:** You're tired of talking to wrong-fit prospects and want to repel the bad fit while attracting your ideal client.

```
I want to position myself as the specialist for [SPECIFIC NICHE/AUDIENCE].

Examples of ideal clients:
- [DESCRIPTION 1]
- [DESCRIPTION 2]
- [DESCRIPTION 3]

What I do NOT want to work on: [TYPES OF WORK YOU HATE]

Write me a 1-paragraph positioning statement (3-4 sentences) that makes it crystal clear who I help and who should look elsewhere. Make it sound confident, not judgmental.
```

## 2. CLOSE & PRICE

### 7. The Objection Handler: Price-Sensitive Prospect

**Use when:** They love the idea but the price is "too high" and you need a reframe, not a discount.

```
I'm selling [SERVICE] for [YOUR PRICE] to [AUDIENCE].

Their objection: "It's too expensive / I need to think about it / We have a tighter budget."

The real value they'll get: [OUTCOME: time saved, revenue gained, cost avoided, problem solved]

In numbers, that's worth: [QUANTIFIED VALUE]

Write me a response that:
- Acknowledges their concern (genuine, not dismissive)
- Reframes the cost as an investment, not an expense
- Shows the math: cost of NOT doing this vs. cost of the solution
- Offers ONE alternative (payment plan, smaller scope, different timeline) — don't throw everything at them
```

### 8. Custom Proposal That Closes

**Use when:** You've had the discovery call and need a written proposal that feels personalized, not templated.

```
Here's what the prospect told me:
- Their problem: [PROBLEM]
- Their goal: [GOAL]
- Their timeline: [TIMELINE]
- Their budget range: [BUDGET]

My solution scope:
- Phase 1: [WHAT I'LL DO]
- Phase 2: [WHAT I'LL DO]
- Timeline: [WHEN]
- Investment: [YOUR PRICE]

Write me a 1-page proposal (in plain English, not corporate jargon) that:
- Restates their problem + goal so it's clear I heard them
- Breaks down the solution in phases (not overwhelming)
- Leads with outcomes, not deliverables
- Ends with a clear next step (sign here, let's start [DATE], schedule kickoff call)

Make it feel like an email, not a PDF that needs a notary.
```

### 9. The Pricing Conversation Script

**Use when:** They ask "So how much does this cost?" and you need to NOT stumble or undersell.

```
I sell [SERVICE] and my base price is [YOUR PRICE].

That price includes: [WHAT'S INCLUDED]

It doesn't include: [WHAT'S EXTRA]

Typical result: [OUTCOME]

Write me a 3-sentence script for when a prospect asks "How much?"

The script should:
- State the price clearly (confidence, no apology)
- Give context (what's included, typical ROI/outcome)
- Leave space for them to ask questions

Make it sound like someone who knows they're worth it.
```

### 10. The "Let's Start" Email

**Use when:** They said yes. Now send them something that confirms next steps, not confusion.

```
They just signed/agreed to:
[SERVICE/PROJECT DESCRIPTION]

We start: [DATE]
Our first step: [FIRST TASK/MEETING]
Timeline: [OVERALL TIMELINE]
They need to send me: [WHAT YOU NEED FROM THEM]

Write me a short "let's go" email that:
- Confirms what they signed up for (so there's no surprise)
- Names the first action (call, document, payment, whatever)
- Makes it dead simple for them to take that action
- Feels genuinely exciting, not procedural
```

### 11. Social Proof Without the Awkwardness

**Use when:** You want to mention past wins without sounding like you're bragging.

```
I've worked with: [TYPE OF COMPANY/PERSON].

Common outcomes:
- [RESULT 1: quantified if possible]
- [RESULT 2]
- [RESULT 3]

Write me a 2-3 sentence "social proof" paragraph I can use in my website footer, email signature, or before a sales call.

It should:
- Mention the types of clients I've helped (not names if they're private)
- Lead with the outcome they cared about
- Sound conversational, not a testimonial
- Make someone think "oh, they know how to do this"
```

### 12. The Follow-Up to "I Need to Think About It"

**Use when:** You've sent the proposal. Radio silence. Time to nudge without being annoying.

```
I sent a proposal for [SERVICE] on [DATE].

The core offer: [WHAT YOU'RE SELLING]
The ask: [WHAT THEY NEED TO DECIDE]

Write me a 4-5 sentence follow-up email that:
- Acknowledges time might have gotten away from them (humans are busy)
- Re-anchors them to ONE key outcome (not a feature list)
- Offers a soft way to say no if this isn't it ("No problem if it's not the right time, just let me know")
- Suggests a 15-min call to answer questions (low-pressure next step)

Make it feel like a peer checking in, not a debt collector.
```

## 3. GET PAID FASTER

### 13. The Invoice That Gets Paid On Time

**Use when:** You're sending an invoice and want to see the money, not a 60-day silence.

```
I'm invoicing [CLIENT] for [SERVICE] on [DATE].
Amount: [YOUR PRICE]
My normal payment terms: [e.g., Due in 15 days, Net 30]

Write me an invoice email (not just an attachment) that:
- Confirms the work/service (what they're paying for)
- Shows the amount clearly
- Names the due date and how to pay (bank transfer, Stripe, etc.)
- Sounds warm, not aggressive

Keep it short — an invoice isn't a relationship letter. Make paying you easy.
```

### 14. The First Payment Reminder (Soft)

**Use when:** Invoice is due tomorrow and they haven't mentioned payment yet.

```
I sent an invoice to [CLIENT NAME] on [DATE] for [SERVICE].
It's due [DATE].
Amount: [PRICE]

The payment method they have: [METHOD: bank transfer, Stripe, etc.]

Write me a quick Slack/email reminder that:
- Assumes they're just busy (genuine, not sarcastic)
- Re-confirms the amount and due date
- Offers to re-send the invoice or set up a payment method if they need
- Keeps it to 2-3 sentences

Sound like a friend reminding them, not a lawyer.
```

### 15. The Late-Payment Nudge

**Use when:** Payment is 5+ days overdue and you need the money but want to keep the relationship.

```
I invoiced [CLIENT] for [SERVICE] on [DATE].
Due date: [DATE]
Amount owed: [PRICE]
Today: [TODAY]
Days overdue: [DAYS]

I still want to work with them, but I need the cash.

Write me a firm but kind email that:
- Reminds them of the amount and how overdue it is (facts, no anger)
- Offers ONE way to fix it fast (payment plan, partial payment, direct bank info)
- Names a deadline ("If I don't hear from you by [DATE], I'll assume...")
- Keeps the door open to future work (but sets boundaries)

Make it clear this is real, not a reminder they can ignore.
```

### 16. Automatic Payment Setup for Recurring Clients

**Use when:** You want repeat clients to pay automatically and stop asking for invoices every month.

```
I have recurring clients who pay me [FREQUENCY: monthly, weekly, etc.] for [SERVICE].
Current setup: [HOW YOU'RE INVOICING THEM NOW]

Write me a short email I can send to repeat clients that:
- Proposes setting up recurring payments (saves them AND me time)
- Explains the benefit (it's automatic, reliable, no more back-and-forth)
- Offers 2-3 payment methods (Stripe subscription, ACH, bank standing order)
- Makes it dead simple for them to set up (one link, short form)

Make it sound like a gift to them, not a trick.
```

### 17. The Payment-Plan Offer (For Big Projects)

**Use when:** Someone wants your work but needs time to pay the full amount.

```
A prospect wants to work on [PROJECT] for [TOTAL PRICE].
They can afford [PARTIAL AMOUNT] now.
Timeline for payment: [THEIR TIMELINE]

I'm okay with a payment plan, but I need [YOUR CONDITIONS].

Write me a payment-plan offer email that:
- Confirms the total scope and price
- Shows the payment schedule clearly (e.g., 50% now, 50% on completion)
- Names what happens if payments are missed (pause work, add interest, etc.)
- Explains why this works for both of us (they get the work, I get paid reliably)

Make it professional, not reluctant.
```

### 18. The "Your Payment Succeeded" Email

**Use when:** Money landed. Use this to lock in next steps and feeling of momentum.

```
[CLIENT] just paid [AMOUNT] for [SERVICE].
Payment date: [DATE]
Next steps in our work: [WHAT HAPPENS NEXT]

Write me a warm receipt/confirmation email that:
- Thanks them (genuine, not robotic)
- Confirms what they paid for (clarity)
- Tells them when they'll see results/deliverables (manage expectations)
- Expresses enthusiasm for the work (make them feel smart for hiring you)

Keep it short but human.
```

## 4. SAVE HOURS

### 19. The Client Onboarding Email Sequence Outline

**Use when:** Every new client asks the same questions and you're explaining the same things over and over.

```
I work with [CLIENT TYPE] on [SERVICE].

Most clients need to know:
- [COMMON QUESTION 1]
- [COMMON QUESTION 2]
- [COMMON QUESTION 3]
- [COMMON QUESTION 4]

Write me a skeleton for a 4-email onboarding sequence (send over days 1-7) that:
- Email 1: Warm welcome + sets expectations
- Email 2: Answers [QUESTION 1] + [QUESTION 2]
- Email 3: Answers [QUESTION 3] + [QUESTION 4]
- Email 4: First win or milestone + what's next

Each email should be 1 short paragraph max. I'll fill in details for each client.
```

- Solution: [WHAT I'LL DO]

- Language that sounds personalized even though it's templated
- A call-to-action that's clear (sign, call, yes/no)

```

They hired me to: [THEIR GOAL]
We've completed: [PHASE/MILESTONE]
Next: [WHAT'S COMING]

Write me a 3-4 sentence check-in email I can send every [TIMEFRAME: week/two weeks/month] that:
- Confirms we're on track (or resets expectations if we're not)
- Reminds them of the end goal (so they stay excited)
- Asks for feedback if they have input
- Keeps the tone collaborative (we're partners, not vendor-client)

Make it quick — this isn't a status report, it's a pulse check.
```

Make them feel like they made a smart decision.

**Use when:** You keep doing the same admin work by hand and want to know what could be automated.

```

```

This helps my clients: [HOW IT APPLIES TO THEM]

### 26. The Referral Incentive Pitch

**Use when:** You want clients to refer you and are willing to reward them for it.

```
I'd love to scale my business via referrals. My typical client is worth [REVENUE] to me per project.

I'm willing to offer [INCENTIVE: cash, discount, free work, etc.] for a successful referral.

Write me a short announcement I can send to past clients and warm contacts that:
- Explains why I love referrals (not generic — tell the truth)
- Names exactly who I'm looking for (so they know who to send)
- Describes the incentive clearly (what they get, when they get it)
- Makes it feel natural (a favor between peers, not a sketch scheme)

Keep it short and genuine.
```

### 27. The Upsell to Existing Clients

**Use when:** A client just paid for Service A and would benefit massively from Service B.

```
A client I just finished [PROJECT A] would be a perfect fit for [PROJECT B / UPSELL SERVICE].

Cost to them: [PRICE]
Benefit to them: [OUTCOME]

Write me a casual message (email, Slack, or brief call script) that:
- Acknowledges we just wrapped something great (build on momentum)
- Mentions the new service almost as an aside (not a hard sell)
- Explains why it makes sense for them specifically (personalized, not generic)
- Offers a small discount or bonus if they decide to move fast (create urgency without pressure)

Make it sound like you're doing them a favor, not hunting a commission.
```

### 28. The "Rate & Refer" Request

**Use when:** You've delivered great work and want a testimonial + referrals without being awkward.

```
I just finished a project with [CLIENT] and I'm really proud of the work.

The outcome: [RESULT]

Write me a message (email or Slack) I can send them that:

Make it clear I'm not desperate, just building.
```

```

- Offers a trial or small pilot if they're unsure

Make it feel like a partnership expansion, not a cash grab.
```
